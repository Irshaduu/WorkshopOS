DATA ONLY, NO LABELS. Three lines of identity, then the four lists, then one line of money. The owner's reasoning is the load-bearing part: *"few times repeatedly see, humans will understand and adapt easily."* A caption is what you need the FIRST time and what costs you every time after, on a page four people open twenty times a day. So position carries the meaning and nothing is announced. WHAT THE 2026-08-28 PASS CHANGED, and what each one earns: · THE TWO DATES ARE LABELLED, with the GAP BETWEEN THEM NAMED. Line 1 carried "06/06/2026 – 08/06/2026", which on a page whose whole rule is that position carries the meaning was the one place position could not carry it: two dates of the same shape side by side do not say which is which. The pill in the middle answers the question the card was actually opened with — how long did we hold this car — which was subtraction the reader was doing. NOT THREE DATES. `paid_date` is tracked and is deliberately absent: measured over the 150 settled cards in the demo set, 149 were settled on the very day they were completed, so a third date would print the same number twice on all but one card. The money line at the foot already refuses to print a figure twice; this is the same rule one card up. · THE JOB CARD NUMBER IS ON THE PAGE. `bill_number` is what the workshop reads out on the phone and what the Job Cards list searches by, and the one screen dedicated to a single card was the screen that never printed it. · THE TWO ACTIONS ARE IN A ⋮ MENU. They were filled buttons pinned to the top-right, the two loudest objects on a screen whose only job is to be read, both of them leaving it — and ~90px off the car's own name at 375px. · EACH SECTION CARRIES ITS OWN SUBTOTAL, and this is the real optimisation. `update_totals()` is `Σ spares.total_price + labour_amount` over both routes, so Job Performed + Inventory Items + Spare Parts total the figure on the money line EXACTLY. The bill stops being something you take on trust. Labour sits in the SECTION heading rather than on a row, because labour is one charge on the card and never a price per line. · THE STATE BANNER IS GONE, folded into the foot of the date card. "Completed" as a full-width amber bar said what a date says better. ON HOLD was added there because it was nowhere: a paused car drew the same page as a running one, on the one screen that claims to say where the car is. ONE COLUMN, not two. The four sections sat side by side for half a day and the owner had them straightened out: a 2×2 makes you read in a Z, and the two columns are unrelated lists of unrelated lengths, so the right-hand one starts wherever the left-hand one happened to end. Down a single column the eye only ever goes one way and every section starts at the same x. It also gives the rows the full width, which is what lets the money sit in its own right-hand column — see `.dv-money-col`. EVERY ROW IS A GRID, not a run-on sentence. What a part IS goes left, what it COST goes right, and the facts about it sit quietly underneath. Before this they were one string — "10/07/2026 – 10/07/2026 · ₹5,727 – ₹7,967" — where the eye had to find the ₹ to know where the dates stopped. Right-aligned and `tabular-nums`, the figures form a column you can run down. Office and Owner only. That is not squeamishness about the data, it is what lets the layout be this dense: line 2 runs mileage, mechanic, customer and phone number together with no captions, and every part sets the workshop's COST beside the customer's price.
