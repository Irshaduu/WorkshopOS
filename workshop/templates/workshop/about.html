What’s in here

The system holds the whole workshop — the cars, the parts, the money and the people. Everything below is what exists today, in plain words. The map above is the same thing as a drawing.

The car’s day

Admitted → settled

Job Card

One car, one card. Everything about a visit lives on it.

  • Only one open card per number plate at a time
  • Six sections, in the order the work happens — Vehicle Details, Customer & Notes (photos live in here), Customer Concerns, Job Performed, Inventory Items, Spare Parts
  • Parts go into two of those, and choosing between them is the whole parts rule below: Spare Parts for anything bought in for that car, Inventory Items for anything taken off your own shelf
  • Labour is one figure for the whole job, not a price per line
  • The floor fills it in and never sees a price — that is enforced on the server, not just hidden

Estimate

A quote you hand over before anything starts.

  • Joined to nothing else — quoting a part moves no stock and no money
  • When the car really comes in, the job card is typed fresh
  • Clearing a line’s name is how you delete it, so nothing is lost to a stray tap

Completed & Invoice

Work finished, and the bill can be settled.

  • The floor marks a car completed — undoing that takes office or an owner
  • That is what moves it into Pending Bills, ready to settle
  • One A4 sheet, both kinds of part in a single list
  • Shelf parts print as what they are, never the brand you buy
  • A settled bill gets a small paid stamp; an unpaid one gets nothing

The settle check

Before you take money, a quiet list of what’s still blank.

  • It never blocks you — it just says what’s missing
  • Worth a look, because a settled bill locks itself
  • It only appears when there is actually something to say

Where you watch it

Screens for the day, not for the books.

  • Dashboard — what’s on the floor now, and how far along
  • Live Report — parts still waiting, who’s holding what, and any bill that went out with gaps
  • Car Profiles — everything a number plate has ever done here

Looking something up

Three ways in. Which is quickest depends on what you already know.

  • Know the car — open Car Profiles. Find the number plate and every visit that car has ever made is listed under it, newest first, with what each one was billed. Usually the shortest way to an old card, and nothing to remember
  • Job Cards — every card in the workshop, searchable and filterable by date, for when you know roughly when rather than which car
  • Estimates — every quote written, this year or all of them

Parts & stock

Two routes

A part reaches a car one of two ways

This is the idea the whole parts side is built on.

  • Bought from a spare shop for that car
  • Taken off your own shelf
  • Either way it is charged once — the day it goes on the car, never the day it was delivered

Spare Shops

What you owe each shop, and what you’ve paid.

  • Every purchase joins that shop’s ledger the moment it is recorded, and stays there for good — paying does not take it off
  • What you owe is everything ever bought from them, less everything ever paid to them
  • A payment clears the oldest purchases first
  • A shop can’t be put away while money is still owed
  • An old shop stays attached to what you already bought from it

Unassigned parts

Things that arrived but aren’t on a car yet.

  • The floor can record one the moment it’s delivered
  • Office fills the price in later
  • Blank price means nobody has said yet — not that it was free

Warehouse

Your own shelf.

  • Stock moves by itself — bills add, job cards take
  • Nobody types a stock number anywhere in the system
  • It can go below zero, and that means a bill is missing — not that you’re running low

Supplies Shops & bills

Where the shelf gets filled.

  • A delivery raises the shelf and what you owe — it isn’t a cost yet
  • The cost lands the day a mechanic takes the part
  • A discount spreads across the bill’s lines, so shelf cost stays honest
  • Bills are usually keyed long after the goods arrive, and that is fine. Date the bill to the day it was delivered, and the parts already taken off the shelf since then pick up their true cost — so a bill entered late still lands the cost in the right month

What a part is called

Two lists that decide the names.

  • Categories — the plain name for a part, and what prints on the customer’s bill
  • Products — the branded thing you actually buy. That name is for you; the customer’s bill shows the category instead
  • Shop catalogue — which products each supplies shop actually sells
  • Take a product off a shop’s catalogue and, if that shop has ever billed you for it or any is still in stock, it is not removed — it stays in that shop’s list marked Deactivated and simply stops appearing on new bills. Old bills are untouched, and you can put it back any time

Keeping an eye on it

Three small screens that answer themselves.

  • Low Stock — anything under a quarter of what you normally keep. Order these
  • A minus is listed on its own, and it means the opposite: more was taken than the shelf was recorded as holding, so a supplies bill has not been entered yet. Enter it and the count corrects itself — don’t reorder these
  • Stock History — who took what, and when
  • Average cost — what the shelf actually paid, worked out again from scratch every time a bill changes

Money coming in

Bills & fleets

Pending Bills

Work finished, money not in yet.

  • A card arrives here the moment somebody marks it Completed — whether the car has already gone or is still standing in the yard waiting to be collected
  • Cards still being worked on are not here. Nothing on them is final yet, and they are on the dashboard the whole time they are with you
  • Fleet cars are not here either; they sit on their own account

Paid Bills

Settled, filed by the day the money came in.

  • A walk-in pays once, at pickup — anything short of the full bill is recorded as a discount
  • Changing the work on an old card does not move it in this list. The discount is simply worked out again so the figures still add up, and the bill stays filed under the day it was actually paid
  • Entering a payment again is different — that is a fresh settlement, so it files the bill under today. Right when you have genuinely collected more money; wrong when you were only correcting the card

Fleet Accounts

Customers who pay later, across many cars at once.

  • One payment spreads over the oldest bills first
  • Pay more than owed and the extra waits as credit for next time
  • Part paid only ever happens here — a walk-in is either settled or not
  • Undoing a payment goes newest first, so nothing gets tangled
  • An account holding unsettled cars can’t be put away

Money going out

Shops, wages & the day-to-day

Cashbook

Rent, power, tea, scrap — the day-to-day.

  • One list, money in and out together
  • Dated by the day the money moved, not the day you typed it
  • A new heading snaps to a spelling already in use, so one cost stays one line

Salary & Advance

Wages, month by month.

  • Advances already handed out come off the settlement
  • Settle the finished month first, then change anyone’s pay
  • Once a newer month is settled, the older one closes for good
  • A settled month’s advances are frozen — in and out

Paying the shops

Clearing what you owe, on their own rhythm.

  • Oldest bills first, on both kinds of shop
  • It settles a debt — it never moves profit
  • The part was already counted the day it went on a car

Discount Audit

Every bill that went out light, in one list.

  • Anything more than ₹3,500 off
  • You’re asked to confirm at the counter before it happens

What you read

Owner only

Profit

Turnover, less what the work cost. The distribution number.

  • The sum is printed on the screen, so you can follow it
  • It says out loud when something is missing — an unsettled wage month, a part with no cost yet
  • Then the same profit again, broken down by what earned it

Cash Tracking

Money moved, not profit.

  • What came in and what went out between the two dates you picked, each rupee counted on the day it really moved
  • Deliberately kept apart from profit, because the two rarely agree: a part becomes a cost the day it goes on a car, while the cash for it leaves whenever that shop is next settled

Deep Analysis

Eight views, one at a time.

  • Mechanics, spare parts, inventory, vehicles, fleet, shops, cashbook, operations

Deletion History

Every permanent delete, with who did it and the reason they gave.

  • There is no restore — bringing old money back would break running balances

Names & tidying up

So one thing stays one thing

Master lists

Car brands, models, spare names and common complaints.

  • For a brand and a model, the list decides the spelling. Type “toyota” or “TOYOTA” on a card and it is saved the way the list spells it, so one make can never turn into three in the reports
  • Spare names and complaints are saved exactly as typed — which is what Data Cleanup is for
  • Renaming a brand or model here reaches the cards already recorded
  • Deleting a name touches no old card; it comes back the next time somebody types it

It learns as you type

Suggestions come from what the workshop has really used.

  • A new name is picked up on its own, no setup
  • Job lines are suggested from the parts already on that card

Data Cleanup

Spare names and complaints, with a count beside each — so a duplicate stands out.

  • These two lists and no others, because these are the names typed straight onto a card and kept exactly as typed. Brands and models tidy themselves, so they are not here
  • Every spare name and every common complaint, and how often each has really been used
  • A name used forty times beside a near-copy used once is usually one thing written two ways — “AC Filter” and “Cabin Filter” for the same part
  • Rename or merge from here and the old cards follow, so both spellings become one line everywhere they are counted
  • Merging is confirmed first, and shows exactly what carries over and what is dropped
  • It moves no money and no stock — only names

Getting in

Accounts & who sees what

Three levels

And the rules are enforced on the server, not just hidden on screen.

  • Owner — everything
  • Office — the day’s work and the money, but not the owner’s screens
  • Floor — the work, and never a price

Signing in

One door, and it names no roles.

  • Office and floor sign in with the username an owner gave them. That is all a staff login holds — no email, no number
  • An owner signs in with their email address — a username will not work, and neither will the workshop’s phone number. That is on purpose: the number is published on the website, and five wrong tries locks an account, so anyone who could name an owner could shut that owner out

If you’re locked out

Five wrong tries locks that account for fifteen minutes.

  • An owner can lift a staff lock straight away from Control Hub
  • There’s a second, looser limit for the whole connection, so one fumbled password on the tablet never locks everyone out

Forgotten password

A six-digit code by email, for owners.

  • The code is in the subject line, so you can read it off the notification
  • It expires, works once, and clears your lockout when you use it
  • Owners cannot reset each other — this is the way back

Devices

Every signed-in device, and where from.

  • You can sign any device out remotely — if one goes missing, or if it leaves with somebody

Control Hub

Owner-only. Logins, staff and security in one place.

  • Create or remove a login, reset a staff password, lift a lock
  • Owner accounts aren’t managed here — that’s the emailed code

The staff roster

Everyone on the payroll, not just mechanics.

  • Mechanics, assistant mechanics, office staff and helpers
  • Only a mechanic or assistant can be put on a job card as the lead
  • People are retired, never deleted — their old job cards stay exactly as they were
  • Retiring someone still holding advances warns you at that moment, not days later

What keeps it honest

Quietly, in the background

The financial lock

A settled bill stops moving.

  • You can unlock one deliberately if you have to
  • The numbers behind it get put straight afterwards
  • A settled card’s photos lock with it — still viewable, not changeable

The seven-day window

Office can undo a recent mistake. Anything older is yours.

  • Counted from when it was typed in, not the date on the money
  • Nobody is blocked — it just comes to you instead, and says so by name

Amounts and dates

Small checks that stop quiet damage.

  • Every typed amount is checked before it is saved
  • A part can’t arrive before it was ordered
  • Nothing that moves money can be dated forward — money dated ahead is a mistyped year far more often than a plan
  • Money is filed by the day it moved, so a late entry lands in the right month

Put away, don’t delete

Shops, fleet accounts and staff are archived, never removed.

  • Their history stays exactly as it was
  • A shop you still owe, or a fleet account that still owes you, is refused outright. Retiring a staff member who is holding advances is allowed, but warns you as you do it
  • A real delete is permanent — and every one that touches money or the workshop’s records is written down, with who did it
  • Quotes and photos are the exception, and stay unlogged on purpose: they are drafts and housekeeping, and an alert that fires for those is one nobody reads

Alerts

The handful of things worth a buzz reach your phone.

  • Anything permanently deleted, any discount over ₹3,500, and every sign-in — an owner’s as well as a staff member’s
  • Anything that changes who can get in: a login created or removed, a staff password changed, an account locked, a password reset
  • Owners only, and never for something you did yourself — so what reaches you is always somebody else
  • A signed-in device stays signed in for weeks, so a sign-in alert means a genuinely new one, not somebody reopening the app
  • Kept short deliberately: a phone that buzzes for everything stops being read

The app itself

Underneath

On your phone

Add it to the home screen and it behaves like an app.

  • No address bar, opens straight to the workshop
  • On an iPhone, alerts only work once it’s installed that way
  • If the connection drops, it says so instead of looking broken

Photos

A car or a part, straight from the camera.

  • One tap per shot — no reviewing, and it tells you if one fails
  • Kept off to one side, so a slow upload never holds up saving a card
  • Kept at least a year. Clearing old ones out is a deliberate job, never something that happens behind your back — and photos on a bill nobody has paid are never cleared, because that is the argument they are evidence in

Backups

Everything but the photos is in one database.

  • That one thing is the whole workshop — every card, ledger, wage and record
  • A full copy can be taken whenever you want one, and the last fourteen are kept
  • Photos are held separately, so they are kept safe separately

Email

Used for one thing only.

  • Password reset codes, and nothing else

Why it behaves the way it does

Five rules, everywhere

Every rupee is counted once, and counted when it happened

Most of the rules on this page come back to this one.

  • A part is a cost exactly once: the day it goes on a car. Not the day it was delivered, not the day the shop was paid
  • So a delivery raises the shelf and raises what you owe, and changes profit by nothing at all
  • And a job’s takings and that job’s parts always land in the same month — whatever month the car was finished in, or paid for
  • Which is what keeps a good month a good month, instead of swinging on when a bill happened to be keyed

Money owed is on exactly one screen

Every debt has one home, and nothing can hide it.

  • A shop or a fleet account still carrying a balance refuses to be put away, rather than disappearing with the money still on it
  • Whatever you archive, tidy or filter, what is owed stays where you can find it

It would rather say “I don’t know” than guess

A number you can’t trust is worse than a gap you can see.

  • A part with no cost recorded yet is reported as unknown — never quietly as free
  • A wage month not settled yet is named on the profit page rather than estimated into it
  • Where a figure is short, the screen says so, in words

The rules are enforced, not just hidden

Anything the screen refuses, the system refuses underneath.

  • The floor is not shown a price, and cannot send one either
  • A settled bill, a closed wage month, an archived shop — each holds where it cannot be worked around
  • Which is what makes the numbers worth reading in the first place

It speaks up before it matters, not after

A warning that arrives late is just a complaint.

  • What is still blank on a bill is shown at the counter, while it can still be fixed
  • And it never stands in your way — a question you are asked every single time is one nobody reads
