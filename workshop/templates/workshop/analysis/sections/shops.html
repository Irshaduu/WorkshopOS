A spare-shop part goes straight onto a car, so what the shop billed IS the cost, on the day of that job. A Supplies Shop bill puts goods on the WAREHOUSE SHELF: it raises the shelf and the payable and is charged nowhere, because the cost lands later, on the day a mechanic draws the part — the Inventory section's "Stock used". Both were labelled "spend" under a footnote defining spend as what the Profit page charges, so the supplies tile made a promise the figure does not keep, and the two numbers (₹85,000 billed against ₹1.88L drawn, on the demo data) could not be reconciled by anybody reading them. Spend is what spare-shop parts cost — they go straight onto a car, so it is the figure the Profit page charges. Billed is what the Supplies Shops delivered onto the warehouse shelf; nothing charges that, because shelf stock becomes a cost when a mechanic draws it — which is Stock used in the Inventory section. Paid is cash that actually left the drawer. The three are not meant to match: stock is bought in one month and used in another, and shops are settled in instalments.
