Same split as Paid Bills and Completed. THE ROW, and why it is shaped this way. An estimate is written fast, often with the customer standing there, so most of its fields are optional and any of them may be blank. The row therefore has to read well in every combination, not just a full one. Two rules do that work: 1. **The headline is whatever identifies the car best.** Brand + model if they were typed, else the registration number, else the estimate number. There is always exactly one big line, never an empty space where one should be. 2. **Nothing blank is announced.** A missing customer prints nothing at all rather than "No customer name" — a row with three grey apologies in it looks broken, and the absence already says everything it needs to. `priced` is the same distinction the printed document makes: ₹0.00 means a quote someone deliberately made for nothing, while an estimate with no figures yet is a draft. Printing "₹ 0.00" for a draft would state a price the workshop never quoted. {% endcomment %} {% comment %} The count rides in the swapped markup rather than being recomputed in the browser: the page shows the number of matches across ALL pages, which the 45 rows on screen cannot tell you. The header pill reads it from here after a search so the two can never disagree. {% endcomment %}
